The current data model is a typed in-repo list. That is the fastest way to get a stable shell online without bringing a CMS into the critical path.

The important part is separation. The content sits in one file, the routes stay predictable, and the rendering logic can later be swapped to MDX with minimal disruption.

## What comes next

- Move posts from TypeScript objects to MDX files.
- Generate RSS and sitemap metadata.
- Add tags, prev-next article navigation, and a denser archive view.

:::info
MDX gives you the full power of React components inside Markdown. You can drop in callout boxes, interactive demos, or custom diagrams — all within a plain `.mdx` file.
:::

## Why not a CMS?

A CMS adds a request boundary between the author and the reader. For a personal site, that boundary is overhead: you need an account, an API key, a webhook, and a fallback for when the service is down.

Files in a repo do not go down. They are version-controlled, diffable, and editable in any text editor. The tradeoff is that you lose a rich editing UI, but for technical writing that is usually not the bottleneck.

:::tip
Keep frontmatter minimal. Only store fields you actually render. Every extra key is a maintenance burden waiting to happen.
:::
